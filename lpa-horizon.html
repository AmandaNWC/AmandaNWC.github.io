RESEARCH NOTES / CLINICAL TRIALS

Lower Lp(a), But No Lower Heart Risk?

Nai-Wen Chi · November 13, 2026 · Literature reading note

This trial studied patients with existing cardiovascular disease. The questions below about LDL cholesterol and earlier treatment are my own interpretation, not conclusions proven by the trial.

Why I Read This Paper

High Lp(a) is linked to a greater risk of heart disease, so I was interested in a large trial of pelacarsen, a drug designed to lower it. The results surprised me. The drug lowered Lp(a) a lot, but patients did not have fewer major cardiovascular events. I wanted to understand why these two results were so different.

What Did the Researchers Find?

The Phase III Lp(a)HORIZON study included more than 8,300 people with high Lp(a) and existing cardiovascular disease. Pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) by about 78%. After a median follow-up of 4.7 years, however, the main cardiovascular outcome was nearly the same in the two groups.

Major cardiovascular events

12.3% with pelacarsen versus 12.1% with placebo.

In other words, a large change in the blood test did not lead to a clear clinical benefit in this study.

My Questions

Two things caught my attention. First, the participants were around 60 years old on average and already had heart or blood vessel disease. I wonder whether lowering Lp(a) much earlier in life could give a different result.

Second, their LDL cholesterol was already quite low, with a median level of about 65 mg/dL. We already have strong evidence that lowering LDL reduces heart risk. Could good LDL control change how much extra benefit patients get from lowering Lp(a)?

What I am still wondering

Does the timing of treatment matter? And would the result look different in people with less well-controlled LDL?

These are questions, not answers from the trial. The study does not prove that LDL is more important than Lp(a), or that lowering Lp(a) cannot help other groups.

My Takeaway

I learned that lowering a risk factor and improving patients' health are not always the same thing. For a new drug, the real test is not just whether a blood marker changes, but whether people actually benefit.

Reference and scope

Cho L, et al. Pelacarsen and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Patients with High Lipoprotein(a). New England Journal of Medicine (2026). Published November 7, 2026.

DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2610159 ↗
