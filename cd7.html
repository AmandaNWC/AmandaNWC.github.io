TARGETED LNPs / T CELLS

Choosing a receptor that carries the cargo inward

Nai-Wen Chi · October 9, 2026 · Literature reading note

Reading note on receptor selection, intracellular delivery, and questions for follow-up experiments.

When choosing a receptor for T-cell delivery, I would want to know what happens after the particle binds. A receptor can be easy to detect on the cell surface without providing an efficient route for cargo to reach the compartment where it works.

What the study reports

Zeng and colleagues compared LNPs targeting CD2, CD4, CD5, CD7, CD8, and a CD4 + CD8 combination under matched conditions in human T cells and PBMCs. CD7 targeting produced the strongest mRNA delivery among the tested options. The authors also demonstrated functional anti-CD20 CAR T-cell generation in humanized mice. Their mechanistic analysis linked delivery efficiency primarily to receptor internalization, with receptor abundance providing a weaker explanation of the ranking. They report that this behavior was largely independent of antibody clone.

The comparison gives me a reason to include trafficking measurements early in a receptor screen. It does not establish a universal ranking for every formulation, cargo, or cell state.

What I would take from the comparison

My interpretation is that target selection needs an intracellular component. I would measure the receptor on the surface, then ask how quickly the particle enters and whether that entry leads to expression. Those measurements describe different parts of the delivery process. A strong signal at one step can leave a downstream bottleneck unresolved.

I would also want to distinguish internalized cargo from material attached to the cell surface. Otherwise, an uptake readout could make a receptor look useful without showing that it actually carries cargo inward. The control matters because it changes what the measurement can support.

The question changes with the cargo

My follow-up question concerns DNA delivery: would the receptor ranking remain the same if productive expression also depended on nuclear access? This is a hypothesis prompted by the study, rather than a result it establishes.

I wonder whether a receptor that performs well for mRNA would still be favored when the endpoint is DNA-driven expression. Faster internalization could help, but I would want to know where the cargo travels after entry and whether differences in cell state change the relationship between entry and expression.

Question to test

Does the receptor that produces the most internalized cargo also produce the most functional expression, and does that relationship change with the payload?

What I would need to check next

A closer examination of the methods and donor-level data would help me assess how surface binding, internalization, and expression were measured. A follow-up comparison would need to account for cell viability, activation state, and particle properties.

Source and scope

Zeng J, Papp TE, Akyianu A, Bahena A, Leo L, Halilovic F, Parhiz H. Rapid receptor internalization potentiates CD7-targeted lipid nanoparticles for efficient mRNA delivery to T cells and in vivo CAR T-cell engineering. Journal of Controlled Release. 2026;396:115043.
