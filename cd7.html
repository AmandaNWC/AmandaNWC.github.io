TARGETED LNPs / T CELLS

Choosing a receptor that carries the cargo inward

Nai-Wen Chi · October 9, 2026 · Literature reading note

Reading note on receptor selection, intracellular delivery, and questions for follow-up experiments.

When choosing a receptor for T-cell delivery, I would want to know what happens after the particle binds. A receptor may be abundant on the cell surface, but that does not tell us whether it helps the cargo reach the right place inside the cell.

What the study reports

Zeng and colleagues compared LNPs targeting CD2, CD4, CD5, CD7, CD8, and a CD4 + CD8 combination under matched conditions in human T cells and PBMCs. CD7 targeting produced the strongest mRNA delivery among the tested options. The authors also demonstrated functional anti-CD20 CAR T-cell generation in humanized mice. The authors found that receptor internalization was more closely linked to delivery efficiency than receptor abundance. This pattern was largely independent of the antibody clone tested.

When comparing receptors, I would also measure where the cargo goes inside the cell. The best receptor in this study may not be the best for another formulation, cargo, or cell state.

What I would take from the comparison

I would measure the amount of receptor on the surface, how quickly the particle enters the cell, and whether the cargo leads to expression. These are separate steps. A particle can enter well and still fail to produce much expression.

I would measure internalized cargo separately from particles still bound to the cell surface. Otherwise, the uptake result could count particles that have not entered the cell.

The question changes with the cargo

I would like to test the same receptors with DNA cargo. DNA needs to reach the nucleus for expression. Would the same receptor still work best? This is my follow-up question; the mRNA results do not answer it.

Faster internalization could help DNA delivery, but I would also track where the cargo goes after entry. I would test whether cell state changes how much expression follows uptake.

Question to test

Does the receptor that brings the most cargo into a cell also lead to the most functional expression? Does the answer change with the cargo?

What I would need to check next

Next, I would read the methods and compare data from individual donors to check how the study measured surface binding, internalization, and expression. In a follow-up experiment, I would also check cell viability, activation state, and particle properties.

Source and scope

Zeng J, Papp TE, Akyianu A, Bahena A, Leo L, Halilovic F, Parhiz H. Rapid receptor internalization potentiates CD7-targeted lipid nanoparticles for efficient mRNA delivery to T cells and in vivo CAR T-cell engineering. Journal of Controlled Release. 2026;396:115043.
