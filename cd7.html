TARGETED LNPs / T CELLS

Choosing a receptor that carries the cargo inward

Nai-Wen Chi · October 9, 2026 · Literature reading note

Reading note on receptor selection, intracellular delivery, and questions for follow-up experiments.

When choosing a receptor for T-cell delivery, I would want to know what happens after the particle binds. A receptor may be abundant on the cell surface, but that does not tell us whether it helps the cargo reach the right place inside the cell.

Why I Read This Paper

When designing nanoparticles to deliver genetic material to T cells, choosing a surface target is an important step. I used to think that a receptor found on many cells might be a good target. But binding to a cell is not the same as delivering cargo inside it. That is why this paper on CD7-targeted lipid nanoparticles caught my attention.

What Did the Researchers Find?

Zeng and colleagues compared nanoparticles targeting CD2, CD4, CD5, CD7, CD8, and a CD4/CD8 combination. In the tested human T-cell systems, CD7 targeting gave the strongest mRNA delivery. They also showed that CD7-targeted nanoparticles could produce functional anti-CD20 CAR T cells in humanized mice.

One finding stood out to me: having more receptors on the cell surface did not necessarily mean better delivery. Instead, how quickly a receptor was brought into the cell was more closely linked to the result. So receptor number and receptor behavior may tell different stories.

My Questions

I would like to know whether the same result applies when the cargo changes from mRNA to DNA. mRNA can be translated in the cytoplasm, while DNA must reach the nucleus before it can be expressed. A receptor that helps mRNA delivery may not work as well for DNA.

Question I would like to test

Does the receptor that brings in the most particles also give the highest level of gene expression?

To answer that, I would compare particles attached to the surface, particles actually taken into cells, and the final protein expression. I would also check cell health, because strong delivery is less useful if it harms the T cells.

My Takeaway

This study changed how I think about target selection. Instead of choosing a receptor mainly because it is common on T cells, I would ask where the cargo goes after binding. Getting into a cell is a useful first step, but successful expression is the result that matters most for a gene-delivery system.

Source and scope

Zeng J, Papp TE, Akyianu A, Bahena A, Leo L, Halilovic F, Parhiz H. Rapid receptor internalization potentiates CD7-targeted lipid nanoparticles for efficient mRNA delivery to T cells and in vivo CAR T-cell engineering. Journal of Controlled Release. 2026;396:115043.
