RESEARCH NOTES / CAR-T

What Makes CAR-T Therapy Successful?

Nai-Wen Chi · October 28, 2026 · Literature reading note

This study examined CAR-T cells manufactured ex vivo. The questions below about in vivo CAR-T are my own interpretation, not findings tested in this study.

Why I Read This Paper

CAR-T therapy can produce durable responses in certain blood cancers, but outcomes vary. A recent Nature Medicine study explored which T-cell characteristics are associated with better outcomes in lymphoma. I read it because of my interests in drug delivery, in vivo CAR-T engineering, and immunotherapy.

What Did the Researchers Find?

The researchers analyzed axi-cel products from 580 patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma and validated key findings in an independent cohort of 203 patients receiving axi-cel-based therapy or brexu-cel.

A higher proportion of naive-like T cells, defined by CCR7 and CD45RA expression, was associated with longer progression-free survival. These markers describe a less-differentiated phenotype; they do not establish that every cell is a true naive T cell.

The association remained significant after accounting for CAR-T expansion and other clinical factors. This was an association analysis: it does not prove that increasing this cell population would improve outcomes.

Why Does This Matter for In Vivo CAR-T?

Conventional ex vivo CAR-T involves modifying T cells outside the body before infusion. In vivo approaches aim to engineer T cells directly inside the patient through targeted delivery.

My research question

If CAR delivery succeeds, how can we ensure that the resulting T cells have characteristics that support effective and durable treatment?

I would want to evaluate differentiation state, functional activity, and persistence alongside delivery efficiency. These are questions for future studies; the clinical findings here do not establish which cells an in vivo platform should target.

My Takeaway

Successful gene delivery is one part of developing an effective cell therapy. This paper encourages me to consider the biological properties of engineered T cells alongside delivery performance. Its ex vivo findings provide a starting point for questions about in vivo engineering, with further evidence needed.

Reference and scope

Budka J, Davis M, et al. Determinants of response to CAR T cell therapy in a multicenter, multi-trial cohort analysis of patients with lymphoma. Nature Medicine (2026). Published October 23, 2026.

DOI: 10.1038/s41591-026-04707-w ↗
