RESEARCH NOTES / CAR-T

What Makes CAR-T Therapy Successful?

Nai-Wen Chi · October 28, 2026 · Literature reading note

This study examined CAR-T cells manufactured ex vivo. The questions below about in vivo CAR-T are my own interpretation, not findings tested in this study.

Why I Read This Paper

CAR-T therapy can work very well for some people with blood cancer, but not everyone has the same response. I was interested in a Nature Medicine paper that looked at the T cells used to make CAR-T products. My work interests include drug delivery and in vivo CAR-T, so I wanted to know what makes an engineered T cell more likely to work.

What Did the Researchers Find?

The researchers studied CAR-T products from 580 patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma. They also checked their main findings in another group of 203 patients. People whose CAR-T products had more naive-like T cells tended to have longer progression-free survival.

These cells were identified using two markers, CCR7 and CD45RA. The markers suggest a less mature T-cell state, but do not prove that every cell is truly naive. The link with better outcomes remained after the researchers considered other clinical factors, including CAR-T expansion.

Importantly, this was an association. The study did not test whether adding more naive-like cells would improve treatment.

My Questions

In conventional CAR-T therapy, T cells are changed outside the body before being given back to a patient. In vivo CAR-T aims to change them directly inside the body. That makes me curious about what happens after delivery.

Question I would like to explore

If we deliver a CAR successfully, will the engineered T cells have the right properties to keep working over time?

I would look at more than the number of cells expressing CAR. I would also want to know their state, activity, and how long they remain in the body. These are my questions, not things the paper tested for in vivo CAR-T.

My Takeaway

Good delivery matters, but it is not the whole story. This paper made me think more about the cells that receive the treatment, not just the carrier that brings it to them. For in vivo CAR-T, both steps seem worth studying together.

Reference and scope

Budka J, Davis M, et al. Determinants of response to CAR T cell therapy in a multicenter, multi-trial cohort analysis of patients with lymphoma. Nature Medicine (2026). Published October 23, 2026.

DOI: 10.1038/s41591-026-04707-w ↗
