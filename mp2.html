NOTE 002 / INTRACELLULAR DELIVERY / HUMAN EX VIVO

What happens after uptake?

Nai-Wen Chi · October 9, 2026 · Research commentary

The MP2 study made me look more closely at the gap between a cellular fluorescence signal and functional expression. The authors developed methylprednisolone-derived ionizable lipids for local mRNA delivery to the colon. Their lead formulation combines delivery activity with an anti-inflammatory lipid component.

A comparison that depends on the cell type

In Caco-2 cells, MP2 and SM102 formulations carrying labeled RNA showed comparable cellular uptake. MP2 showed less overlap of RNA fluorescence with endolysosomal markers. The authors interpret the lower colocalization as evidence of improved escape. They also report more Galectin-9 puncta after MP2 treatment in Caco-2, RAW 264.7, and primary mouse colonic epithelial cells, supporting increased endosomal membrane disruption. These results are described in the main text with references to Supplementary Figures 8 and 9.

The uptake result is specific to the model. In RAW 264.7 macrophages, MP2 uptake was higher than SM102 uptake. I would therefore avoid summarizing the study as “uptake was unchanged.” The relationship between entry and subsequent trafficking differed across the tested cell types.

My interpretation is that uptake, membrane disruption, and expression should be measured together. Reduced colocalization and galectin recruitment support an escape mechanism, but neither directly counts intact, translation-competent RNA reaching the cytosol. The expression readout helps connect the imaging observations to a functional outcome.

What the formulation control can establish

The Methods describe MP2 LNPs using DOPE, cholesterol, and DMG-PEG2k at a lipid molar ratio of 20:30:40:0.75. The SM102 comparator uses DSPC with a ratio of 50:10:38.5:1.5. The comparison therefore changes multiple formulation variables.

I read the reported delivery advantage as a formulation-level result. To isolate the contribution of the MP2 lipid, I would want a matched composition study, followed by optimization of each formulation. Those comparisons answer different questions: which ingredient changes the mechanism, and which complete formulation performs best?

The carrier also contributes to the pharmacology

The authors tested lipid-associated anti-inflammatory activity in LPS-stimulated RAW 264.7 cells. MP2 lipid and MP2 LNPs suppressed IL-6 and TNF-α. For therapeutic experiments, they loaded IL-35 mRNA and evaluated acute TNBS colitis and chronic disease in IL-10 knockout mice. They also compared MP2-IL-35 with SM102-IL-35 and SM102-IL-35 plus free methylprednisolone.

That last control asks whether the benefit can be reproduced simply by adding the parent drug to another delivery formulation. It helps examine the combined contribution of lipid structure and delivery, though it does not fully separate every pharmacological and distribution variable. Repeat-dose development would need to consider exposure to both the expressed protein and the active lipid or its metabolites.

What the human tissue adds

Figure 5 connects delivery to biological activity in human colonic biopsies. The paper reports expression in epithelial, endothelial, and immune populations, and changes in inflammatory and regulatory T-cell subsets after MP2-IL-35 treatment of patient tissue. This asks whether relevant human tissue can respond to the formulation and cargo.

The biopsy experiments are ex vivo. They do not reproduce rectal administration, whole-body distribution, or repeated exposure in a patient. I would use them as human tissue evidence while keeping those unanswered questions visible.

Question to test

For a DNA payload, would expression correlate more closely with nuclear delivery than with total cellular uptake or membrane-disruption markers?

This is my follow-up hypothesis, not a finding from the MP2 study. Together with the CD7 receptor comparison, it suggests a measurement sequence I would want to discuss: entry, intracellular routing, cargo availability at its site of action, and function. Changing the cell type, formulation, or payload could change which step limits the outcome.

Source and scope

Cao D, Tian M, Yu C, et al. Anti-inflammation lipid nanoparticles enable localized mRNA therapy for inflammatory bowel disease. Nature Communications. 2026. Article in Press.
