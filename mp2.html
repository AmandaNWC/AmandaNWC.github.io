LOCAL mRNA DELIVERY / FORMULATION CONTROLS

When uptake and expression differ

Nai-Wen Chi · October 2026 · Literature reading note

Selected findings from the article’s results and methods, followed by my interpretation and research questions.

The MP2 study made me ask how much of the treatment effect comes from better mRNA delivery and how much comes from the carrier itself. I would want measurements and controls that separate these effects.

What the study tested

Cao and colleagues developed glucocorticoid-derived ionizable lipids for local mRNA delivery. Their lead MP2 formulation carries a methylprednisolone-derived lipid and was combined with a thermosensitive hydrogel for rectal administration. Figure 1 compares delivery in cells and in a colitis mouse model; later experiments test IL-35 mRNA and human colonic biopsies.

These experiments answer different questions. Cell experiments help select a carrier. Mouse experiments test delivery in a disease model. Human biopsies show how human tissue responds, but do not establish safety or efficacy in patients.

Uptake does not explain every expression difference

The Results section reports similar uptake of labeled RNA for MP2 and SM102 in Caco-2 cells. MP2 also showed lower RNA–endosomal-marker colocalization. In RAW 264.7 cells, MP2 showed greater uptake than SM102. The main text describes these results with Supplementary Figure 8. The uptake comparison therefore depends on the cell model.

I would measure uptake, intracellular location, and expression in the same comparison. Lower colocalization could mean that RNA moves differently inside the cell. It does not, by itself, measure how much intact, functional RNA reaches the cytosol. The article also reports galectin recruitment as another indicator of membrane disruption. I would compare these results with expression data; one image would not be enough to show successful delivery.

A formulation comparison is more than a lipid swap

In Preparation and characterization of LNPs, the glucocorticoid-derived formulations use DOPE, whereas the SM102 comparator uses DSPC. Their lipid ratios also differ. The study compares complete formulations with several differences. It cannot show how much of the result comes from changing the ionizable lipid alone.

I would keep the other components the same where possible and test whether delivery still differs. This would help explain the lipid’s contribution. It is a different question from which complete formulation works best.

Separate carrier activity from cargo activity

The MP2 lipid has anti-inflammatory activity of its own. Figures 3 and 4 include MP2 carrying a reporter mRNA alongside MP2 carrying therapeutic mRNA. Figure 5 then examines expression and immune-cell responses in human colonic biopsies ex vivo.

These controls help separate the effects of the encoded protein from those of the carrier. For repeat dosing, I would measure exposure to both the mRNA and the active lipid. The biopsy experiments cannot fully show how a treatment would be administered, where it would go in the body, or whether it would be safe over time.

Question to test

If a formulation improves expression and the carrier also has pharmacological activity, which matched controls can show how much each contributes to the treatment effect?

This complements the CD7 receptor note: receptor choice affects entry, while the formulation and where the cargo goes inside the cell affect what happens next.

Source and scope

Cao D, Tian M, Yu C, et al. Anti-inflammation lipid nanoparticles enable localized mRNA therapy for inflammatory bowel disease. Nature Communications. 2026. Published article ↗

Source locations: Figures 1, 3–5; Results, “Design and development of LNPs for local mRNA delivery to colon”; Methods, “Preparation and characterization of LNPs” and “Cell assays”; Discussion. Observations attributed to Supplementary Figure 8 were checked against the main article’s description, not against a separately reviewed supplementary file. Proposed comparisons are my interpretation.
